Heirloom Recipes

A LEGACY OF FLAVOUR FROM SOUTHERN COURTYARDS

The name Rivaya draws inspiration from timeless living traditions, heirloom recipes, and the deep emotional warmth of authentic home-cooked food. Long before supermarkets and mass manufacturing, traditional pantries relied on the seasonal harvest of raw hard mangoes, wild gooseberries, sour limes, and fragrant cold-pressed oils.

Pickling was an art: watching the monsoon clouds clear, selecting hard raw mangoes whose seed shells had just hardened, roasting fenugreek seeds in heavy iron vadas until they release an earthy nutty perfume, and pouring golden cold-pressed gingelly oil that seals the freshness naturally.

“True pickle cannot be hurried. It must sleep in the shade, wake to the sun, and absorb each spice until the oil turns into molten gold.”

Pure Earth Ingredients

THE INTEGRITY OF WHAT WE SOURCE

Great taste comes from uncompromising ingredient honesty. We partner directly with regional agricultural growers across South India who follow non-synthetic methods.

  • Cold-Pressed Gingelly & Mustard Oils: Unrefined oils extracted on wooden chekku mills, containing natural antioxidants.
  • Natural Rock & Solar Sea Salt: Pure unprocessed salts that draw out juices without chemical sharpness.
  • Unadulterated Whole Spices: Guntur red chillies, Malabar black pepper, high-curcumin turmeric, and pungent asafoetida.
Our Daily Discipline

THE PHILOSOPHY OF HANDCRAFTING

In our kitchens, machinery is kept to a minimum. Human intuition, sight, touch, and smell guide every single stage of preparation.

Sun-Cured on Clean Terraces

Our papads are hand-pressed and laid on pure white muslin cloths across open rooftops to absorb the golden South Indian sunlight.

Stone Grinding & Small Batches

Spices are crushed gently on stone mortars to prevent heat buildup, preserving volatile aromatic oils and fragrant zest.

Sealed in Clean Glass

We use heavy food-grade glass jars and airtight tins. Pickles breathe and age naturally in glass without leaching plastics.
